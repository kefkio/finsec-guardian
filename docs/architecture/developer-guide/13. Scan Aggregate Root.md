# Scan Aggregate Root

## Architecture Design Document

Document ID: ARCH-DOM-001
Component: Scan Aggregate Root
Layer: Domain
Package: scanner/domain/entities/scan.py
Status: Blueprint (Pre-Implementation)

## 1. Purpose

The Scan aggregate represents the complete lifecycle of a smart contract security analysis.

It is the central business object within the scanning bounded context and acts as the Aggregate Root for all artifacts produced during a scan, including findings, reports, and scan metadata.

A Scan is not merely a database record. It represents an active business process that progresses through well-defined lifecycle stages while enforcing domain invariants (rules) and maintaining consistency across related entities.

## 2. Problem Statement

A smart contract security scan consists of multiple coordinated activities:

   - accepting a smart contract for analysis,
   - executing one or more analyzers,
   - collecting security findings,
   - calculating overall risk,
   - generating a report,
   -  recording completion status.

Without an Aggregate Root, these activities become fragmented across unrelated entities, making it difficult to enforce business rules and maintain consistency.

The Scan aggregate provides a single consistency boundary for the entire scanning process.

## 3. Business Definition

A Scan represents one execution of the FinSec Guardian analysis engine against a single smart contract.

Every scan has:

   -  one smart contract,
   -  one lifecycle,
   -  zero or more findings,
   -  zero or one report,
   -  one status.

Everything produced by the scanner belongs to exactly one scan.

## 4. Responsibilities

The Scan aggregate owns the following responsibilities.

   - Lifecycle Management
   - create a scan
   - start execution
   - complete execution
   - fail execution
   - cancel execution (future)
   - archive completed scans (future)
   - Finding Management
   - accept new findings
   - prevent duplicate findings (future)
   - maintain ownership of findings
   - expose finding statistics
   - Report Management
   - associate the generated report
   - ensure only one report exists
   - State Management
   - track current status
   - record timestamps
   - validate legal state transitions
   - Aggregate Consistency

Maintain all business invariants associated with the scanning process.

## 5. Non-Responsibilities

The Scan aggregate deliberately does not perform the following responsibilities.

   - execute Slither
   - parse Solidity
   - generate fingerprints
   - calculate hashes
   - perform database operations
   - expose REST endpoints
   - read files
   - orchestrate infrastructure

These belong to the Application and Infrastructure layers.

The aggregate root owns all child entities.

No child entity should exist independently of its parent scan.

## 7. Aggregate Boundary

The aggregate boundary contains:

   - Scan

   - SmartContract

   - Finding

   - Report

Objects outside the aggregate may reference the Scan by identity but must not modify child entities directly.

## 9. State Transition Rules

Current State Allowed Transition

   - CREATED	RUNNING
   - RUNNING	COMPLETED
   - RUNNING	FAILED
   - COMPLETED	ARCHIVED
   - FAILED	ARCHIVED

All other transitions are invalid.

## 10. Business Invariants

The aggregate must enforce the following rules.

   ### Invariant( Rule) 1

A scan must reference exactly one smart contract.


   ### Invariant 2

A scan must always have a valid status.


   ### Invariant 3

Findings may only be added while the scan is running.


   ### Invariant 4

Completed scans are immutable.


   ### Invariant 5

Failed scans cannot transition to completed.


   ### Invariant 6

Completion time must be greater than or equal to start time.


   ### Invariant 7

Only one report may be attached.


   ### Invariant 8

Every finding belongs to exactly one scan.


   ### Invariant 9

A report may only be attached after analysis has completed.

## 11. Public Interface

The aggregate should expose business operations rather than CRUD-style methods.

   - start()

   - complete()

   - fail()

   - add_finding()

   - attach_report()

   - finding_count()

   - has_findings()

   - is_running()

   - is_completed()

   - is_failed()

Notice that every method represents a business action.

## 14. Why Scan Is the Aggregate Root

A Finding has no business meaning without the execution that produced it.

A Report is the output of a completed scan.

A SmartContract may participate in many scans over time.

The only object that naturally owns the complete business transaction is the Scan.

Therefore it is the Aggregate Root.

## 15. Design Principles Applied

Principle	Application
Domain-Driven Design	Scan models the core business transaction.
Aggregate Pattern	Scan protects consistency across related entities.
Single Responsibility Principle	Responsible only for scan lifecycle and consistency.
Open/Closed Principle	New business rules can be added without changing external contracts.
Tell, Don't Ask	Consumers invoke business operations instead of manipulating state directly.
Rich Domain Model	Behavior resides in the aggregate rather than application services.

## 17. Future Evolution

The aggregate has been intentionally designed to support future capabilities without altering its core responsibilities.

Potential enhancements include:

Incremental scans
Distributed analyzer execution
Scan retries
Partial scan recovery
Multi-language smart contracts
Scan versioning
Historical comparison of scan results
Event sourcing
Domain events (e.g., ScanStarted, ScanCompleted, FindingAdded)
Architect's Note #49 – The Scan Aggregate Is the Heart of the Scanning Domain

The Scan aggregate models the entire lifecycle of a security analysis. Rather than treating findings, reports, and smart contracts as isolated records, it establishes a single consistency boundary that owns the business transaction from start to finish. Its primary responsibility is not orchestration but protection of business invariants: valid state transitions, ownership of findings, report association, and lifecycle consistency.

External concerns such as analyzer execution, persistence, and HTTP communication remain outside the aggregate and are coordinated by the application layer. This separation keeps the domain model focused on business rules while allowing infrastructure to evolve independently.
